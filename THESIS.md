# THESIS — Measurement-Lag Instability in Adaptive Optimizers

Dated 2026-10-30. Extracted from planning doc v0.5 Part 2 (planning/adam-lag-instability_planning.md, MD5 b055fc5963206e5334d8bf35c1cbca07); Date section per JAE direction 2026-10-30.

**Title.** Measurement-Lag Instability in Adaptive Optimizers. **Subtitle (Amendment A2):** A Fast–Slow Divergence Early-Warning Signal for Loss Spikes, Tested Against Deployed Alarms on Public Training Logs.

**The claim.** Adam-family optimizers steer training off exponential trailing averages of gradient statistics (β₁ ≈ 0.9 → ~10-step memory; β₂ ≈ 0.999 → ~1,000-step memory) and feed those averages back into the parameters that generate the next gradients. This is a measurement-feedback loop of the class analyzed in *The Measurement Trap*: a lagged estimate of a persistent variable driving a controller that changes the variable. Two consequences follow and are tested here. *(β₂ framing superseded in part by Amendment A2.)*

- **A (early warning).** The divergence D(t) = M_f(t) − M_s(t) between a fast and a slow trailing average of the per-step gradient norm turns positive before loss spikes, with a lead that exceeds that of the rolling-statistics alarms in current use at matched false-alarm rate. Mechanism: *Adaptation Rate Theorem* (lead ≈ (w_s − w_f)/2 at midpoint) plus *Persistence-Sign Theorem* (gradient-norm autocorrelation is positive and decaying → D predicts the forward level with positive sign).
- **B (stability boundary).** The stability of the second-moment loop is governed by the persistence φ of the squared gradient norm, the effective window W = 1/(1−β₂), and the feedback strength b_g set by the learning rate, through the exponential-kernel criterion of *The Measurement Trap* §3.5. Runs the criterion classifies as unstable exhibit more spikes/divergence than runs it classifies as stable, at matched everything else. *(Scope restated by Amendment A1, 2026-10-30 — see The gap.)* *(Superseded by Amendment A2: Claim B is a characterization.)*

**Why it matters.** Loss spikes force checkpoint rewinds and batch skips (PaLM: >20 rewinds; a documented 65B run: +30 days, +129 MWh). A signal that fires ahead of them, and a pre-flight check that says which (β₂, LR) pairs are inside the stable region, converts wasted compute into avoided compute. Sized in the session of 2026-09-14 at low single-digit percent of frontier training compute — material to a tool, not to a hyperscaler's budget; stated as such.

**The gap.** Prior work (a) documents that gradient-norm spikes precede loss spikes (OLMo 2; ZClip; SPAM), (b) attributes spikes to Adam's assumption of time-domain gradient independence (Molybog et al. 2023), and (c) characterizes instability via curvature at the "edge of stability" (Cohen et al. 2021, 2022) and small-scale proxies (Wortsman et al. 2023). None states the fast–slow divergence as the alarm with a lead-time bound, and none gives a closed-form window/persistence/gain criterion for the second-moment loop. The prior-art scan (planning/adam-lag-instability_planning.md Part 4; scan recorded at Phase 0) must confirm this gap survives before any data work. *(Original wording, kept visible; superseded in part by Amendment A1 below.)*

**Amendment A1 (2026-10-30) — the gap after the Phase 0 prior-art scan** (PRIOR_ART.md, same commit, MD5 56b971bfb23dd94127ee1675eee14551). The second-moment lag mechanism is established prior art: Adam's second-moment estimate falls behind the squared gradients and that lag destabilizes training (Shazeer & Stern 2018; Wortsman et al. 2023; Bai et al., ICML 2026, "Adaptive Preconditioners Trigger Loss Spikes in Adam"; Tang, Chen & Liu 2026, "Beyond Quadratic Loss: The Stability Phase Diagram of Adam"). Bai et al. state the resulting boundary in curvature terms (preconditioned-Hessian eigenvalue against 2/η); Tang, Chen & Liu fit an empirical (β₁, β₂) boundary. **Claim B is therefore restated:** this paper does not propose a new mechanism; it proposes a Hessian-free, closed-form, observable-only predictor of that established mechanism — a criterion computed from quantities already in a training log (persistence φ of the squared gradient norm, W = 1/(1−β₂), and the learning-rate gain b_g) that classifies (β₂, LR) settings before the runs. Claim A stands as written; its scope relative to the deployed current-versus-EMA z-score tests (themselves fast–slow divergences with a one-step fast window) is stated in PRIOR_ART.md §4.

**The single result that would falsify it.**
- Claim A is falsified if, on the OLMo-0424 run (the designated primary), the divergence detector's median lead over recorded loss spikes does not exceed the rolling-z-score baseline's median lead at matched false-alarm rate (DESIGN §K). This is also the KILL RULE for the project. *(Superseded by Amendment A2: best of four baselines; k ladder.)*
- Claim B is falsified if, across the pre-registered (β₂, LR) grid, the criterion-classified unstable group's spike rate is not greater than the stable group's (one-sided Mann-Whitney, α = 0.05), or if the criterion's continuous diagnostic b_g·S fails to rank-order spike rate (Spearman ≤ 0). *(Withdrawn by Amendment A2; replaced by a reporting commitment.)*

**Amendment A2 (2026-10-30, pre-freeze; JAE decisions) — Phase 1 design findings carried into the thesis.** Source: DESIGN.md at commit ab5dd47 (MD5 95781d186dc0f0ec6cd74a4c662397f3) §D.0, §D.2, §D.3, §D.4.1, and DECISIONS.md entry 2.

- **(a) Claim A falsifier (supersedes the Claim A falsifier line).** Claim A is falsified if, on the OLMo-0424 run (the designated primary), the divergence detector's median lead over recorded loss spikes does not exceed that of the **best of four baselines** — rolling z-score, fixed threshold, ZClip EMA z-score, CUSUM — at matched false-alarm rate (1 per 10,000 steps), judged on the per-resample minimum contrast (DESIGN §D.3 E2, §K). This is the KILL RULE.
- **(b) β₂ framing (supersedes "β₂ ≈ 0.999 → ~1,000-step memory" as the typical case).** Adam's second-moment memory depends on β₂: W = 1/(1−β₂) is 1,000 steps at β₂ = 0.999 but 20 steps at β₂ = 0.95, which is what every Leg A run used (OLMo-0424 and OLMo 2 configs). Leg A tests Claim A's early warning independent of β₂ — the detector reads the gradient norm, not the optimizer state. Claim B is examined on Leg B's β₂ grid, which includes 0.95.
- **(c) Insufficient events.** Under the pre-registered spike-definition ladder (k = 4, then k = 3 once), if OLMo-0424 has fewer than 5 eligible events at both rungs, the outcome is INSUFFICIENT EVENTS: the project closes with a null note on event count, which neither passes nor kills Claim A (DESIGN §D.2 P-KLAD, §K).
- **(d) Claim B re-scoped (supersedes Claim B and its falsifier).** The exponential-kernel stability boundary of The Measurement Trap does not map cleanly onto Adam's second-moment loop in observable-only terms: Adam's feedback acts multiplicatively through the step size and runs through local curvature, its second moment includes the current gradient, and the uniform-kernel quantities S and π²/2 have no exponential-kernel counterpart (DESIGN §D.4.1). No mapping is constructed. **Claim B is now a characterization:** how often loss spikes across a pre-registered β₂ × learning-rate grid on a 124M-parameter model, and how well the published exponential-kernel robustness index b_g*(exp) = (1−φ)/α + φ (computed from the measured persistence φ of the squared gradient norm and α = 1−β₂) orders those cells within each learning rate. **The Claim B falsifier is withdrawn and replaced by its reporting commitment:** the 24-cell table of spike rate, divergence flag, φ and b_g*(exp); spike rate by β₂ and by learning rate; Spearman of spike rate against b_g*(exp) across cells and Kendall τ within each learning rate, each with a permutation interval — reported as characterizations, with no pass/fail (DESIGN §D.4 E6, E7). Leg B executes whenever §K does not stop the project (E2 SUPPORT or INCONCLUSIVE); it is not dropped when Claim A passes, and its GPU cost is accepted now (JAE 2026-10-30).
- **(e) Citation (supersedes "The Measurement Trap §3.5" in the Claim B text and the Archetype line).** The exponential-kernel closed form is **The Measurement Trap, Appendix A.4 (PRF-A4), EQ-5/EQ-6**; §3.5 of that paper is the cross-disciplinary correspondence with Campbell & Jessop.
- **(f) Archetype (supersedes the Archetype line's wording; the archetype is unchanged).** Empirical-with-verified-theory. The paper proves no original theorem; The Measurement Trap Appendix A.4 is used as a descriptive index, not as a mapped criterion. The Phase-5a pass is the empirical-archetype pass.
- **(g) Subtitle.** "A Fast–Slow Divergence Early-Warning Signal for Loss Spikes, Tested Against Deployed Alarms on Public Training Logs." Claim B's characterization is described in the abstract, not the subtitle.

**Amendment A3 (2026-10-30, pre-freeze; design-stage adversarial review, JAE decisions) — supersedes the stated parts of A2(a) and of "The claim".** Source: verification/design_review.md; DESIGN.md §D.2, §D.3 E2, §K as amended the same day.

- **(a) Claim A falsifier and kill rule (supersedes "at matched false-alarm rate (1 per 10,000 steps), judged on the per-resample minimum contrast" in A2(a)).** Claim A is falsified if, on the OLMo-0424 run, the divergence detector's **mean** lead over the eligible loss spikes (a missed spike counts as lead 0) does not exceed that of the best of the four baselines at matched false-alarm rate, where the false-alarm rate is the share of steps outside every spike's scan window spent in alarm (headline 1 %), judged on the point estimate of the minimum contrast over the four baselines; the per-resample distribution of that minimum separates SUPPORT from INCONCLUSIVE. This is the KILL RULE (DESIGN §D.3 E2, §K).
- **(b) Mechanism sentence (supersedes "Mechanism: Adaptation Rate Theorem (lead ≈ (w_s − w_f)/2 at midpoint)" in "The claim", A).** The Adaptation Rate Theorem (Thm 3.1) shows that after a level change the fast average crosses a threshold ahead of the slow average, by (w_s − w_f)/2 = 48 steps at the midpoint for the (32, 128) pair. That motivates watching their difference; it does not bound the divergence detector's lead over a loss spike, which is what E2 measures. The Persistence-Sign Theorem clause is unchanged.
- **(c) What counts as support.** E1 (the sign check) is a consistency check on the Divergence Theorem and is never counted as support for Claim A. E8 (early warning on the Leg B runs) is a characterization and does not combine with E2.
