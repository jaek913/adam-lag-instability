# THESIS — Measurement-Lag Instability in Adaptive Optimizers

Dated 2026-10-30. Extracted from planning doc v0.5 Part 2 (planning/adam-lag-instability_planning.md, MD5 b055fc5963206e5334d8bf35c1cbca07); Date section per JAE direction 2026-10-30.

**Title.** Measurement-Lag Instability in Adaptive Optimizers. **Subtitle (Amendment A2):** A Fast–Slow Divergence Early-Warning Signal for Loss Spikes, Tested Against Deployed Alarms on Public Training Logs.

**The claim.** Adam-family optimizers steer training off exponential trailing averages of gradient statistics (β₁ ≈ 0.9 → ~10-step memory; β₂ ≈ 0.999 → ~1,000-step memory) and feed those averages back into the parameters that generate the next gradients. This is a measurement-feedback loop of the class analyzed in *The Measurement Trap*: a lagged estimate of a persistent variable driving a controller that changes the variable. Two consequences follow and are tested here. *(β₂ framing superseded in part by Amendment A2.)*

- **A (early warning).** The divergence D(t) = M_f(t) − M_s(t) between a fast and a slow trailing average of the per-step gradient norm turns positive before loss spikes, with a lead that exceeds that of the rolling-statistics alarms in current use at matched false-alarm rate. Mechanism: *Adaptation Rate Theorem* (lead ≈ (w_s − w_f)/2 at midpoint) plus *Persistence-Sign Theorem* (gradient-norm autocorrelation is positive and decaying → D predicts the forward level with positive sign).
- **B (stability boundary).** The stability of the second-moment loop is governed by the persistence φ of the squared gradient norm, the effective window W = 1/(1−β₂), and the feedback strength b_g set by the learning rate, through the exponential-kernel criterion of *The Measurement Trap* §3.5. Runs the criterion classifies as unstable exhibit more spikes/divergence than runs it classifies as stable, at matched everything else. *(Scope restated by Amendment A1, 2026-10-30 — see The gap.)* *(Superseded by Amendment A2: Claim B is a characterization.)*

**Why it matters.** Loss spikes force checkpoint rewinds and batch skips (PaLM: >20 rewinds; a documented 65B run: +30 days, +129 MWh). A signal that fires ahead of them, and a pre-flight check that says which (β₂, LR) pairs are inside the stable region, converts wasted compute into avoided compute. Sized in the session of 2026-09-14 at low single-digit percent of frontier training compute — material to a tool, not to a hyperscaler's budget; stated as such.

**The gap.** Prior work (a) documents that gradient-norm spikes precede loss spikes (OLMo 2; ZClip; SPAM), (b) attributes spikes to Adam's assumption of time-domain gradient independence (Molybog et al. 2023), and (c) characterizes instability via curvature at the "edge of stability" (Cohen et al. 2021, 2022) and small-scale proxies (Wortsman et al. 2023). None states the fast–slow divergence as the alarm with a lead-time bound, and none gives a closed-form window/persistence/gain criterion for the second-moment loop. The prior-art scan (planning/adam-lag-instability_planning.md Part 4; scan recorded at Phase 0) must confirm this gap survives before any data work. *(Original wording, kept visible; superseded in part by Amendment A1 below.)*

**Amendment A1 (2026-10-30) — the gap after the Phase 0 prior-art scan** (PRIOR_ART.md, same commit, MD5 56b971bfb23dd94127ee1675eee14551). The second-moment lag mechanism is established prior art: Adam's second-moment estimate falls behind the squared gradients and that lag destabilizes training (Shazeer & Stern 2018; Wortsman et al. 2023; Bai et al., ICML 2026, "Adaptive Preconditioners Trigger Loss Spikes in Adam"; Tang, Chen & Liu 2026, "Beyond Quadratic Loss: The Stability Phase Diagram of Adam"). Bai et al. state the resulting boundary in curvature terms (preconditioned-Hessian eigenvalue against 2/η); Tang, Chen & Liu fit an empirical (β₁, β₂) boundary. **Claim B is therefore restated:** this paper does not propose a new mechanism; it proposes a Hessian-free, closed-form, observable-only predictor of that established mechanism — a criterion computed from quantities already in a training log (persistence φ of the squared gradient norm, W = 1/(1−β₂), and the learning-rate gain b_g) that classifies (β₂, LR) settings before the runs. Claim A stands as written; its scope relative to the deployed current-versus-EMA z-score tests (themselves fast–slow divergences with a one-step fast window) is stated in PRIOR_ART.md §4.

**Completeness check (every segment, every route, before any analysis):** the set of logged steps must be contiguous between the segment's first and last step (count of rows = last − first + 1, no duplicate steps within a segment), and the column `optim/total_grad_norm` must be present by NAME. A segment failing this check is not used; if A1 cannot be assembled to full per-step resolution by routes 1–3, A1 is recorded as unavailable, E2 cannot run, and §K's grading day records that fact (D.3 §K).

**A1 assembly (construction pin P-SEG):** segments are ordered by first step. Where two segments overlap in step (a restart from a checkpoint), the rows of the **later** segment are kept for the overlapping steps, because training resumed from the checkpoint and the later segment is the trajectory that continued. Sensitivity: keep-earlier rule, reported in E4. **Rewind events (design review 2026-10-30, F5):** spike events are enumerated on every segment, including the portion of an earlier segment that a restart superseded. An event whose time lies in a superseded portion is a *rewind event*: it is retained, its scan window and every detector score are computed on that earlier segment's own series (which shares history with the kept trajectory up to the checkpoint step), and it is reported with a rewind flag and count. The assembled (later-wins) series is used for every other event and for the false-alarm denominator. Rewind events enter the k-ladder event count. Sensitivity: rewind events excluded, reported in E4. Gaps between segments (steps present in no segment) are recorded; no window (fast, slow, spike baseline, scan) is computed across a gap — windows restart after a gap and the minimum-history rule (D.2) applies again.

**Pre-clip assertion (per run):** `optim/total_grad_norm` is the global gradient norm computed before clipping in allenai/OLMo `olmo/optim.py` (verified at main 090253d, DECISIONS entry 2). At PRV the April-2024 training commit recorded in each run's config is checked for the same order; a run where the logged norm is post-clip is flagged and reported separately, never pooled.

## D.2 Operators (Leg A)

All operators are causal (use data at or before step t only). Steps are optimizer steps.

- **Observable:** g(t) = `optim/total_grad_norm` at step t (pre-clip); y(t) = ln g(t). A non-positive or non-finite g(t) is counted (D.6 item 5) and is **not a gap**: y(t) at that step is carried forward from y(t−1) for every window and EMA, so every window length below counts steps, and steps equal observations (design review 2026-10-30, F15).
- **Loss:** L(t) = `train/CrossEntropyLoss` at step t.
- **Fast/slow averages:** simple moving averages of y over the last w_f = 32 and w_s = 128 steps (ratio 4, Divergence Theorem Thm 14 band). Sensitivity pairs (16, 64) and (64, 256), reported, no verdict.
- **Divergence:** D(t) = SMA_32(y)(t) − SMA_128(y)(t).
- **Gaps and state (design review 2026-10-30, F15):** a gap is a run of steps present in no segment (D.1). At each gap every window statistic (D, the spike baseline, B1), the B3 EMA state (re-initialized from the first 25 post-gap steps), the B4 accumulator and its median/MAD history, and the D_div percentile history reset; the 1,000-step minimum history applies again from the first post-gap step. Nothing else resets state.
- **Detector score D_div:** P_D(t) = empirical percentile of D(t) among all D(s), s ≤ t, in the assembled series; minimum history 1,000 steps (no score before). Alarm when P_D(t) ≥ θ_div.
- **Persistence-sign prediction (E1, fixed before any correlation is computed):** predicted sign of Corr(D_t, y_{t+16}) = sign[R̄(h, h+w_f) − R̄(h+w_f, h+w_s)] at h = 16 (The Divergence Theorem, Theorem 8b, eq. 63), with R̄(a, b) = (1/(b−a))∫ₐᵇ ρ_Y(τ)dτ (Definition 13, eq. 61). Discrete estimate: R̄(a, b) = mean of the sample ACF ρ̂_y(τ) over integer lags τ = a … b inclusive; ρ̂_y is computed on A1's assembled y at lags 0–256, each lag using all step pairs that do not cross a gap. Expected: positive. Scope: Theorem 8b assumes weak stationarity; y has a slow upward drift on OLMo-0424 (OLMo 2 paper §3), so E1 is labelled a consistency check under an approximate-stationarity condition.
- **Spike definition (mechanical):** a loss spike at step t if L(t) > SMA_128(L)(t−1) + k·σ_128(L)(t−1), where SMA_128(L) and σ_128(L) are computed over the finite values of L in the trailing 128 steps; a NaN/inf L(t) counts as exceeding the threshold and is excluded from later baselines. **Dedup (chained) (design review 2026-10-30, F18):** a spike is merged into the current event if it lies within 200 steps of the most recently merged spike; the event time t_e is the first step of the event (dedup 200, frozen 2026-10-30).
- **k ladder (construction pin P-KLAD, JAE 2026-10-30):** k = 4 is the primary rung (frozen 2026-10-30). The number of eligible events on A1 is computed from the loss series and the eligibility rule alone, BEFORE any detector score is computed. If A1 has fewer than 5 eligible events at k = 4, the secondary rung k = 3 applies, once. If A1 still has fewer than 5 eligible events at k = 3, the outcome is INSUFFICIENT EVENTS (D.3 E2, §K). The rung that resolves on A1 is used for every Leg A run and for E8, and the paper reports which rung resolved. No other k is ever used.
- **Baselines** (each produces a scalar score s_b(t); alarm when s_b(t) ≥ θ_b):
  - **B1 rolling z-score:** s_1(t) = (y(t) − mean_50(y)) / sd_50(y), trailing 50-step window. Window 50 is a **construction pin P-B1** (not attributed to any tool); sweep {25, 50, 100}, reported, no verdict.
  - **B2 fixed threshold:** s_2(t) = y(t).
  - **B3 ZClip EMA z-score (documented defaults, ZClip arXiv 2504.02507 / PyPI `zclip`):** on g (not y), EMA mean μ_t and EMA variance v_t with α = 0.97, initialized from the first 25 steps of the series and of each post-gap restart (documented warm-up); s_3(t) = (g(t) − μ_{t−1}) / sqrt(v_{t−1}). Statistics are updated with the raw g(t) (monitor mode, no clipping) — **construction pin P-B3**; sweep: update with ZClip's adjusted value instead, reported, no verdict. The documented decision threshold z = 2.5 is also reported as an un-matched operating point.
  - **B4 CUSUM (trainscope 1.10.0 default `ChangePointDetector`, source kaelvalen/trainscope @ 0651b25):** robust z(t) = (y(t) − median_200(y)) / (1.4826·MAD_200(y)) over the trailing 200 observations, minimum 30 observations; positive accumulator S⁺(t) = max(0, S⁺(t−1) + z(t) − k) with slack k = 1.0; s_4(t) = S⁺(t); accumulator reset to 0 after each alarm and at each gap. trainscope applies this to loss; here it is applied to y so every detector reads the same observable, and only the upward side is used because the target is a rise in gradient norm — **construction pin P-B4**; sweep: two-sided as in trainscope, and applied to g instead of y, reported, no verdict. The documented decision threshold h = 6.0 is also reported as an un-matched operating point.
- **Alarm state (design review 2026-10-30, JAE decision on F2):** a detector is in alarm at step t when its score ≥ θ. False-alarm rate and lead are both defined on alarm state, not on onsets.
- **False-alarm rate (duty cycle; JAE 2026-10-30, replaces "onsets per 10,000 steps"):** FA = (number of out-of-window scored steps in alarm) / N_out, where N_out is the number of scored steps outside every event scan window — the scan windows of ALL events, eligible or not, are excluded from both count and denominator (F18). **FA grid:** {0.5 %, 1 %, 2 %, 5 %}; **headline FA = 1 %**.
- **Threshold matching (construction pin P-FA; F4):** for each detector and each FA target, n* = round(FA_target × N_out). The candidate thresholds are the distinct score values on out-of-window scored steps; θ = the smallest candidate v with #(out-of-window scored steps with score ≥ v) ≤ n*. Alarm-step count is monotone in θ, so every detector is matched at the same integer count n* except where tied score values make the count fall below n*; the realised count and any shortfall are reported per detector.
- **Scan window and lead (P-MISS, P-CENS; F1, F2):** for an event at t_e the scan window is [t_e − 128, t_e]. A detector's lead = t_e − (first step in the window at which it is in alarm). If it is already in alarm at t_e − 128 (and at t_e − 129), the event is **left-censored** for that detector: lead = 128, flagged censored. A detector with no alarm step in the window has lead 0 (miss). Per detector the paper reports: mean lead (the contrast statistic), median lead, detection rate, miss count and censored count. Sensitivity (E4): censored lead = 0; censored events excluded for every detector.
- **Event eligibility:** an event is eligible if every detector has a defined score over its whole scan window (at least 1,000 steps of post-gap history and no gap inside the window). Ineligible events are counted and reported; their scan windows are still excluded from the FA count (F18).
- **Control (E5; F6):** circular-shift null on D_div's alarm-state series (uniform random offset in [1,000, N − 1,000] steps, 1,000 draws), which keeps alarm density and clustering and breaks alignment with the real events; plus a 128-step block shuffle of y, reported as a second characterization.
- **Deseasonalization:** none (training-step series have no seasonal component). **Subsampling:** none — every logged step is used.

## D.5 Synthetic validation suite (committed and ALL PASS before any hashed-data run)

- **Grid and pass criteria (F16).** Synthetic y(t) = AR(1) with φ_y ∈ {0.5, 0.8, 0.9, 0.95, 0.98}; 200 replicates per cell; all RNG per D.7 P-RNG. **Oracle (F10):** for each planted series the expected lead of every detector is the lead the pipeline's own operators give on the same planted series with the noise removed; the oracle C_min is computed the same way. A cell passes if |recovered mean lead − oracle mean lead| ≤ 8 steps for every detector and the recovered C_min has the oracle's sign in ≥ 95 % of replicates. verify.py tolerance for deterministic quantities: 1e-6 relative.
- Planted effect: spikes preceded by ramps of length L ∈ {64, 100, 150, 200} steps; the whole pipeline (assembly → detectors B1–B4 and D_div → duty-cycle threshold matching → leads → C_min → rule) must meet the pass criteria above.
- Planted null: spikes with NO precursor ramp; D_div must not beat the best baseline (false-support rate ≤ 0.05 at the realised event count), including cells where every detector's detection rate at headline FA is between 30 % and 50 % (F1).
- Planted decision flip: ramps shorter than the raw-reading baselines' response; the rule must return REFUTE.
- Dynamic range: lead distributions separate at planted L differences of 50 steps; a planted-ties probe for C_min; a planted-cluster probe (spikes 200–400 steps apart) exercising left-censoring (P-CENS) and chained dedup.
- k ladder: the suite exercises both rungs (k = 4 and k = 3) and the INSUFFICIENT EVENTS branch, and confirms the event count is computed before any detector score.
- Regime span (Standard v1.9.11): event counts {5, 10, 20, 40} × the φ_y grid above; the run reports which cell the realised A1 data lands in. If A1's realised φ̂_y falls outside [0.5, 0.98], the grid is extended by the next value in {0.3, 0.99} by dated amendment before any INCONCLUSIVE is interpreted.
- Alignment control (E5): the flag must trip on planted precursor-free spikes and must not trip at planted L = 100.
- Assembly probes: overlapping segments with a superseded tail containing a spike (rewind event), gaps, duplicate steps, a dropped non-finite g, a missing `optim/total_grad_norm` column, and a point-sampled (incomplete) segment — each must be handled as D.1/D.2 state.
- Leg B: synthetic AR(1) g² with known φ at gradient-noise levels spanning the GPT-2 reference batch to beyond the 98k-token level (D.4.2); the pipeline must recover planted spike-rate differences between cells and a planted ordering against b_g\*(exp) in which the planted φ values reorder at least two cells per LR column relative to β₂ (F9); planted-ties probe for E7; divergence probes (NaN, and a sustained +2.0-nat excursion) for P-DIV; the run reports which noise cell the realised Leg B data lands in.
- Broken fixture: verify.py must fail on a corrupted hash, an out-of-tolerance value, and an unsigned CIC flag.
