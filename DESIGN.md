## D.0 Governing facts and scope

- **No data has been touched.** All A1–A6 staging happens after the public `prereg-v1` commit (DECISIONS entry 1). Everything below is fixed before any hashed input exists.
- **Realised β₂ of every Leg A run is 0.95** (OLMo-0424 and OLMo 2 7B/13B configs: AdamW betas [0.9, 0.95]; second-moment window W = 1/(1−β₂) = 20 steps; global clipping max_grad_norm = 1.0). Consequences: (i) Leg A tests Claim A's early warning *independent of β₂* — the detector reads the gradient norm, not the optimizer state; (ii) Claim B's mechanism is tested only on Leg B's β₂ grid (D.4), which includes 0.95; (iii) the window pair (32, 128) is longer than the optimizer's own 20-step second-moment memory, which is stated in the Methods and probed by E4.
- **Scope of Claim A** (PRIOR_ART §4): the deployed current-versus-EMA tests (ZClip, SPAM, trainscope z-score) are fast–slow divergences with a one-step fast window. This paper's contribution is the smoothed fast window (w_f = 32, r = w_s/w_f = 4, inside the Divergence Theorem's Theorem 14 band r* in [3, 7]) plus the Adaptation Rate Theorem lead bound. E2 tests whether smoothing beats the raw-reading tests at matched false-alarm rate; if it does not, that is a reportable finding against Claim A.
- **Scope of Claim B** (THESIS Amendment A1): a Hessian-free, closed-form, observable-only predictor of the established second-moment lag mechanism — not a new mechanism. **Pre-freeze finding (2026-10-30, D.4.1):** The Measurement Trap's exponential-kernel boundary (Appendix A.4, EQ-5/EQ-6 — not §3.5, which the planning doc mis-cited) does not map cleanly onto Adam's second-moment loop in observable-only terms, so the D.4 fallback is applied now: Leg B carries no Claim B verdict; E6 and E7 are characterizations. THESIS.md records this as Amendment A2(d).

## D.2 Operators (Leg A)

All operators are causal (use data at or before step t only). Steps are optimizer steps.

- **Observable:** g(t) = `optim/total_grad_norm` at step t (pre-clip); y(t) = ln g(t). Non-positive or non-finite g(t) is dropped and counted (D.6).
- **Loss:** L(t) = `train/CrossEntropyLoss` at step t.
- **Fast/slow averages:** simple moving averages of y over the last w_f = 32 and w_s = 128 steps (ratio 4, Divergence Theorem Thm 14 band). Sensitivity pairs (16, 64) and (64, 256), reported, no verdict.
- **Divergence:** D(t) = SMA_32(y)(t) − SMA_128(y)(t).
- **Detector score D_div:** P_D(t) = empirical percentile of D(t) among all D(s), s ≤ t, in the assembled series; minimum history 1,000 steps (no score before). Alarm when P_D(t) ≥ θ_div.
- **Persistence-sign prediction (E1, fixed before any correlation is computed):** predicted sign of Corr(D_t, y_{t+16}) = sign[R̄(h, h+w_f) − R̄(h+w_f, h+w_s)] at h = 16 (The Divergence Theorem, Theorem 8b, eq. 63), with R̄(a, b) = (1/(b−a))∫ₐᵇ ρ_Y(τ)dτ (Definition 13, eq. 61). Discrete estimate: R̄(a, b) = mean of the sample ACF ρ̂_y(τ) over integer lags τ = a … b inclusive; ρ̂_y is computed on A1's assembled y at lags 0–256, each lag using all step pairs that do not cross a gap. Expected: positive. Scope: Theorem 8b assumes weak stationarity; y has a slow upward drift on OLMo-0424 (OLMo 2 paper §3), so E1 is labelled a consistency check under an approximate-stationarity condition.
- **Spike definition (mechanical):** a loss spike at step t if L(t) > SMA_128(L)(t−1) + k·σ_128(L)(t−1). Spikes within 200 steps of a prior spike are merged into one event (dedup 200, frozen 2026-10-30); the event time is the first step of the event. NaN/inf loss at a step counts as exceeding the threshold.
- **k ladder (construction pin P-KLAD, JAE 2026-10-30):** k = 4 is the primary rung (frozen 2026-10-30). The number of eligible events on A1 is computed from the loss series and the eligibility rule alone, BEFORE any detector score is computed. If A1 has fewer than 5 eligible events at k = 4, the secondary rung k = 3 applies, once. If A1 still has fewer than 5 eligible events at k = 3, the outcome is INSUFFICIENT EVENTS (D.3 E2, §K). The rung that resolves on A1 is used for every Leg A run and for E8, and the paper reports which rung resolved. No other k is ever used.
- **Baselines** (each produces a scalar score s_b(t); alarm when s_b(t) ≥ θ_b):
  - **B1 rolling z-score:** s_1(t) = (y(t) − mean_50(y)) / sd_50(y), trailing 50-step window. Window 50 is a **construction pin P-B1** (not attributed to any tool); sweep {25, 50, 100}, reported, no verdict.
  - **B2 fixed threshold:** s_2(t) = y(t).
  - **B3 ZClip EMA z-score (documented defaults, ZClip arXiv 2504.02507 / PyPI `zclip`):** on g (not y), EMA mean μ_t and EMA variance v_t with α = 0.97, initialized from the first 25 steps (documented warm-up); s_3(t) = (g(t) − μ_{t−1}) / sqrt(v_{t−1}). Statistics are updated with the raw g(t) (monitor mode, no clipping) — **construction pin P-B3**; sweep: update with ZClip's adjusted value instead, reported, no verdict. The documented decision threshold z = 2.5 is also reported as an un-matched operating point.
  - **B4 CUSUM (trainscope 1.10.0 default `ChangePointDetector`, source kaelvalen/trainscope @ 0651b25):** robust z(t) = (y(t) − median_200(y)) / (1.4826·MAD_200(y)) over the trailing 200 observations, minimum 30 observations; positive accumulator S⁺(t) = max(0, S⁺(t−1) + z(t) − k) with slack k = 1.0; s_4(t) = S⁺(t); accumulator reset to 0 after each alarm. trainscope applies this to loss; here it is applied to y so every detector reads the same observable, and only the upward side is used because the target is a rise in gradient norm — **construction pin P-B4**; sweep: two-sided as in trainscope, and applied to g instead of y, reported, no verdict. The documented decision threshold h = 6.0 is also reported as an un-matched operating point.
- **Alarm onset:** the first step of each run of consecutive steps with score ≥ θ. Only onsets count (for every detector).
- **False-alarm rate:** alarm onsets per 10,000 steps falling outside every scan window. **FA grid:** {0.5, 1, 2, 5} per 10,000 steps; **headline FA = 1 per 10,000** (frozen 2026-10-30).
- **Threshold matching (construction pin P-FA):** for each detector, θ is swept over the empirical quantiles of its score; the operating θ for a target FA is the one whose realised FA is closest to the target from below (≤ target); ties go to the larger θ.
- **Scan window and lead:** for an event at t_e, the scan window is [t_e − 128, t_e]. Lead = t_e − (first alarm onset in the scan window). An event with no alarm onset in its window has lead 0 (no warning) — **construction pin P-MISS**; the miss count and miss rate are reported per detector, beside its median lead. Left-censoring (an alarm already active at t_e − 128) is reported per Adaptation Rate Theorem §5.2 conventions.
- **Event eligibility:** an event is eligible if every detector has a defined score over its whole scan window (i.e., at least 1,000 steps of history and no gap inside the window). Ineligible events are counted and reported.
- **Control:** block-shuffle null on y (block 128 steps, 1,000 draws): D_div's lead ordering under permutation, per the Adaptation Rate Theorem shuffle-control convention (E5).
- **Deseasonalization:** none (training-step series have no seasonal component). **Subsampling:** none — every logged step is used.

## D.3 Experiments (Leg A) — classification, severity, report form

- **E1 — Persistence-sign check on A1.** Type: hypothesis test (sign). Rule (outcome partition): **SUPPORT** if the realised sign of Corr(D_t, y_{t+16}) equals the D.2 predicted sign AND the one-sided block-permutation p ≤ 0.05 in the predicted direction; **REFUTE** if the realised sign is opposite AND the one-sided block-permutation p ≤ 0.05 in that opposite direction; **INCONCLUSIVE** otherwise, including when the predicted criterion R̄(h, h+w_f) − R̄(h+w_f, h+w_s) is exactly 0 (no prediction). A true null (ρ ≈ 0) therefore maps to INCONCLUSIVE, not to a coin-flip verdict. Report: verdict + ρ with block-permutation z (1,000 permutations of 128-step blocks of y). Severity: near-identity on persistent y; labelled a consistency check.
- **E2 — Lead-time comparison on A1 (PRIMARY; carries §K).** Type: comparative/benchmark (verdict). Statistic: for each baseline b, C_b = median lead(D_div) − median lead(B_b), all at headline FA; C_min = min over b in {1, 2, 3, 4} of C_b. Uncertainty: 1,000 joint resamples of eligible events, each drawn with replacement (events are ≥ 200 steps apart and treated as independent; sensitivity: resampling blocks of 3 consecutive events, reported in E4); every detector's lead is taken on the same resampled events; C_min is computed **within each resample** (per-resample minimum contrast, Standard v1.9.11), never by comparing marginal intervals. Outcome partition (every result maps to exactly one label):
  - **SUPPORT:** C_min > 0 and the 5th percentile of the resampled C_min > 0 (90 % two-sided interval excludes zero).
  - **INCONCLUSIVE:** C_min > 0 but the 5th percentile ≤ 0; reported with the measured detection probability at the observed effect (Standard v1.9.11). Leg B proceeds.
  - **REFUTE (= §K KILL):** C_min ≤ 0.
  - **INSUFFICIENT EVENTS:** fewer than 5 eligible events on A1 at both rungs of the k ladder (P-KLAD) → no verdict on Claim A (neither SUPPORT nor REFUTE); reported with the event count at each rung and the suite's power at that count; §K closes the project.
  Also reported: per detector, the median lead with the miss count (and miss rate) beside it; the k-ladder rung that resolved; lead distributions, censoring rates, the full FA-grid frontier per detector (figure), and the per-resample argmax probability over {D_div, B1, B2, B3, B4}. Severity: quantified by the synthetic suite (D.5) at the realised event count.
- **E3 — Replication on A2–A6.** Type: replication (verdict). Rule: on runs with ≥ 3 eligible events, SUPPORT if the sign of C_min matches E2's on a strict majority of those runs; REFUTE if it matches on a strict minority; INCONCLUSIVE on a tie (a tie is not a null); runs with < 3 events are reported descriptively. A2/A3 are stabilized runs and may have few events; that is reported, not repaired.
- **E4 — Sensitivity.** Type: robustness (no verdict; qualifies E2). Window pairs (16, 64), (64, 256); construction-pin sweeps P-B1, P-B3, P-B4, P-SEG; event-block resampling. Every sensitivity variant re-matches all thresholds to the same FA targets.
- **E5 — Shuffle control.** Type: boundary/characterization (report form). Procedure: shuffle y only, in 128-step blocks (1,000 draws); keep the loss events from the real L; re-match D_div's threshold to headline FA on each shuffled series. Reporting commitment: D_div's median lead and the share of events with lead > 0 (the advance-ordering rate), real vs shuffled (median and 90 % range over draws); a flag trips if the shuffled median lead ≥ the real median lead (the "lead" would be window arithmetic with no data content).

### §K — Kill rule (pre-registered)

- **D.4.1 Criterion source and mapping assessment (2026-10-30).** Source: The Measurement Trap, **Appendix A.4 (PRF-A4)** — not §3.5 (§3.5 is the cross-disciplinary correspondence with Campbell & Jessop). As published: the exponential kernel m(t) = α·x(t−1) + (1−α)·m(t−1) with α = 2/(W+1); companion matrix rows (φ, −b_g) and (α, 1−α); characteristic polynomial **EQ-5:** λ² − (φ + 1 − α)λ + φ(1−α) + α·b_g = 0; boundary φ(1−α) + α·b_g = 1, i.e. **EQ-6:** b_g*(exp) = (1 − φ(1−α))/α = (1−φ)/α + φ. The cumulative persistence memory **EQ-2** S = (1−φ^W)/(1−φ) and the threshold **EQ-1** b_g·S > π²/2 belong to the **uniform** kernel only. **Assessment: the A.4 boundary does not map cleanly onto Adam's second-moment loop in observable-only terms**, for three reasons: (1) in A.4 the measurement feeds back as an additive linear term −b_g·m on the measured variable; in Adam, v_t acts multiplicatively through the step size η/√v_t, and its effect on the next squared gradient runs through local curvature, so any linearized gain depends on the Hessian — which contradicts the Hessian-free scope of Claim B and is not readable from training logs; (2) timing: A.4's kernel uses x(t−1), while Adam's v_t = β₂·v_{t−1} + (1−β₂)·g_t² includes the current g_t², which changes the characteristic polynomial; (3) E7's diagnostic b_g·S is a uniform-kernel quantity with no counterpart in A.4. No mapping is constructed here. **Consequence (D.4 fallback, applied pre-freeze):** Leg B carries no Claim B verdict; E6 and E7 are characterizations; THESIS.md's Claim B falsifier is withdrawn by THESIS Amendment A2(d).
- **D.4.2 Threat to resolution from P-BATCH (stated so the design-stage review sees it).** At 98,304 tokens per step the gradient noise is several times that of the GPT-2 reference batch (~0.5M tokens). Higher noise raises the spike floor in every cell and may compress the separation in spike rate between cells the A.4 index ranks as fragile and robust. The Leg B synthetic suite (D.5) therefore spans gradient-noise levels from the reference batch up to and beyond the 98k-token level, and the run reports which noise cell the realised Leg B runs land in; a realised level outside the span means the resolution claim does not transfer and is re-measured before E6 is interpreted.

## D.5 Synthetic validation suite (committed and ALL PASS before any hashed-data run)

- Planted effect: synthetic y(t) with AR(1) persistence in the measured range, planted spikes preceded by ramps of length L in {25, 50, 100, 200}; the whole pipeline (assembly → detectors B1–B4 and D_div → threshold matching → leads → C_min → rule) must recover lead(D_div) ≈ L − (w_s − w_f)/2 within tolerance and the planted margin over each baseline.
- Planted null: spikes with NO precursor ramp; D_div must not beat the best baseline (false-support rate ≤ 0.05 at the realised event count).
- Planted decision flip: ramps shorter than the raw-reading baselines' response; the rule must return REFUTE.
- Dynamic range: lead distributions separate at planted L differences of 25 steps; a planted-ties probe for C_min.
- k ladder: the suite exercises both rungs (k = 4 and k = 3) and the INSUFFICIENT EVENTS branch, and confirms the event count is computed before any detector score.
- Regime span (Standard v1.9.11): event counts {5, 10, 20, 40} and AR(1) persistence spanning the plausible range; the run reports which cell the realised A1 data lands in; outside the span → resolution re-measured before any INCONCLUSIVE is interpreted.
- Assembly probes: overlapping segments, gaps, duplicate steps, a missing `optim/total_grad_norm` column, and a point-sampled (incomplete) segment — each must be caught by D.1's checks.
- Leg B: synthetic AR(1) g² with known φ at gradient-noise levels spanning the GPT-2 reference batch to beyond the 98k-token level (D.4.2); the pipeline must recover planted spike-rate differences between cells and a planted ordering against b_g\*(exp); planted-ties probe for E7; the run reports which noise cell the realised Leg B data lands in.
- Broken fixture: verify.py must fail on a corrupted hash, an out-of-tolerance value, and an unsigned CIC flag.
